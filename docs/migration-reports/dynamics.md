## Substantive corrections and evidence

1. **Double-pendulum gravity:** MatrixFormalism's first gravity component used `m2*g*l2` although the distal mass is supported through link 1. It now uses `m2*g*l1`. A numerical central difference of the stated potential agrees with the implemented `G=-grad(V)` at the actual initial state (`rtol=1e-8`), its mass matrix is positive definite and the acceleration satisfies `M a = C+G+Q`. `numpy.linalg.solve` replaces explicit inversion. Context7 `/websites/numpy_doc_2_4` supplied the current [solve API](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/generated/numpy.linalg.solve.html).
2. **Kelvin model topology:** Both bilingual chapters described a parallel-spring/Maxwell system but used the step responses of a series spring followed by a Voigt branch. The topology, diagram, constitutive equation and code now consistently describe the latter. Its relaxation time is `b/(ks+kp)` and creep time is `b/kp`. The actual Euler arrays were compared with analytic step responses using an absolute tolerance of 0.005; both language versions pass. Infinite-stiffness limits and the ideal Voigt force impulse are addressed in the original exercise solutions. This is a derivation from series/parallel force and deformation compatibility, not a date-based update.
3. **Variable moment arm:** The original data's numerical angles are −120…10 degrees despite `inDegrees=no`. They map to this guide's angle with `theta=deg2rad(q+180)`. The previous expression converted a radian angle by `pi/180` again. A varying moment arm also requires `L(theta)=L0-integral(r(theta),theta0,theta)`, not `L0-r(theta)*(theta-theta0)`. The restored spline uses its antiderivative and refuses extrapolation. At 70°, 90°, 120° and 170°, finite differences of implemented length match `-r(theta)` to `rtol=1e-7`; reference length, range rejection and finite simulation states also pass. Context7 `/scipy/scipy` verified [CubicSpline](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.interpolate.CubicSpline.html), antiderivative and extrapolation behavior.
4. **Stationary action:** Least action is not always a minimum. For a harmonic oscillator and variation `epsilon*sin(pi*t/T)`, the action difference is `epsilon²*(m*pi²/T-k*T)/4`, negative for sufficiently long intervals; higher-frequency variations can remain positive. The revised explanation distinguishes stationarity from minimization. The [Feynman action lecture](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/II_19.html) was opened and the counterexample evaluated independently.
5. **Jump force and work:** Flight-baseline force is subtracted before quiet-standing body mass is computed, avoiding inconsistent force/mass baselines. Net-force work equals kinetic-energy change; GRF work additionally accounts for gravitational potential change. Measured final values are computed inside collapsed answer panels with explicit boundaries. The full original paper's Table 1 was inspected from `courses/refs/Dowling93JABvert_jump.pdf`; it lists A–s, 19 entries despite referring to 18 parameters. The schematic problem now states its piecewise knots so the final velocity/height are reproducible.
6. **Underdetermined or impossible original Problems:** A 100 kg physical pendulum with COM 1 m from the pivot cannot have a 1 s small-amplitude period at g=10: the formal inferred inertia is 25.3303 kg m², below `mr²=100`; the minimum feasible period is 1.987 s. This is identified rather than silently reporting the unphysical result. Missing mass, initial velocity and body-segment conventions elsewhere are retained as parameters. The body-marker problem states the anthropometric model and reports the computable COM and joint centers.
7. **Fourier conventions:** Real cosine/sine coefficients have harmonic mean-square contribution `(a²+b²)/2`; a complex coefficient has contribution `abs(c)²`, doubled when its negative-frequency partner is folded into a one-sided spectrum. DC remains undoubled. The original `a0` convention is now consistent across the real and complex forms. Phase uses `atan2`, and zero-amplitude phase is undefined. The plotted negative-frequency amplitudes are nonnegative and even; division by zero is removed. Actual arrays pass symmetry/finiteness and one-sided power tests, with the truncated square-wave power between 0.95 and 1. Official [MIT Fourier-series material](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-03sc-differential-equations-fall-2011/pages/unit-iii-fourier-series-and-laplace-transform/fourier-series-basics/) was checked.
8. **EMG filtering and onset:** Context7 `/scipy/scipy` verified [butter](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.butter.html) and [sosfiltfilt](https://docs.scipy.org/doc/scipy/reference/generated/scipy.signal.sosfiltfilt.html). N=2 band-pass gives order 4 per pass and effective order 8 forward/backward; the squared magnitude gives about −6 dB at the designed cutoff. The notebook now explains noncausality and edge effects. Context7 had no matching detecta library after two resolve attempts, so the [maintainer repository](https://github.com/demotu/detecta) and actual `detect_onset.py` signature were inspected instead. `detecta>=0.0.5` is declared. Context7 `/websites/matplotlib_stable` verified that [specgram](https://matplotlib.org/stable/api/_as_gen/matplotlib.pyplot.specgram.html) returns PSD by default, with frequency-density scaling; plots now use that terminology.
9. **Stabilography statistics:** N samples separated by `1/fs` span `(N-1)/fs`; that span now matches the N−1 increments in path length. Sample SD uses `ddof=1`, matching the displayed formula. The norm of directional mean speeds differs from mean resultant speed. Demeaning does not establish stationarity, and correlated COP samples do not automatically have independent-normal 95% prediction coverage. The [original PhysioNet dataset](https://physionet.org/content/hbedb/1.0.0/) was checked for sampling, duration and COP units.
